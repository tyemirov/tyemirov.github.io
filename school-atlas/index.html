Sources & methodology

What does the Value index mean?

It estimates how much community quality your family gets for its annual spending. A higher index means a higher community score, a lower family cost, or both. School rank is shown separately and is not part of this index.

Value index = community score × 64,877 ÷ annual family cost

The community score averages six AARP categories on a 0–100 scale. Family cost includes local 3-bedroom rent, other household spending and school costs for the selected number of children. The fixed 64,877 factor puts the ratio on a readable scale; the index has no dollar unit and is not a percentile or a score out of 100.

Example: a community score of 60 with a $100,000 annual family cost gives an index of 38.9. The same score at $80,000 gives 48.7: the same community quality for less money.

≈ marks an estimate; ≤ marks a ceiling when additional costs remain unpriced. This is a planning comparison before financial aid. For boarding schools, we compare annual family costs in dollars instead, since the family stays at its current home. The detailed assumptions and source links follow below.

01

Who is included

The default view contains the first 100 public schools in national-rank order with documented ordinary residential entry at high-school level, requiring no lottery or selective admission. Every higher-ranked school through the cutoff has an inclusion or exclusion decision. Review the complete admission audit ↗

The Lottery/selection tab contains the first 100 lottery or selective-entry public schools in audited national-rank order, through national #102. The complete 353-school admission audit remains the screening evidence; it is not the displayed group. Each tab contains 100 schools, selected by rank before considering community-data availability. The Local public, Lottery/selection, and Private tabs are mutually exclusive; no school appears in more than one tab. Private schools are the first 100 campus-based entries in Niche rank order (publisher ranks #1–#107); seven online schools are excluded. Their original publisher ranks remain unchanged. Private schools appear in a separate view because public and private positions use different ranking populations and methods. A nonselective middle-school feeder alone does not qualify a school with selective high-school entry. Optional transfers and specialized programs may have separate rules. At schools combining resident and selective programs, the published rank reflects the combined population.

Education, moving up

03

Family cost and value

For public and private day schools: annual cost = 12 × local 3-bedroom gross rent + other household spending + children × school costs + household school fees. Value index = six-category community score × 64,877 ÷ that annual cost. The value index is unitless; annual family cost is shown separately in dollars. The fixed scale is a comparison aid, not literal welfare or a rental quote. Public tuition is $0. All 300 school-campus ZIPs use FY2027 HUD Small Area Fair Market Rent benchmarks; covered utilities are included. Campus ZIP is a nearby-rental proxy, not an attendance boundary or proof that a suitable rental is available. There is no metro or state rent substitution.

The other-spending default is $52,956: BLS 2024 three-person consumer-unit spending of $92,205 minus education $2,585, cash contributions $1,992, personal insurance/pensions $13,302, all shelter $17,555, and covered energy/water utilities $3,815. Health and vehicle insurance, food, transportation, phone/internet and household goods remain. This residual stays at national 2024 prices; a regional all-items price index is not applied. It is an editable benchmark, not your household’s actual costs. BLS household table ↗ · HUD rent methodology and data ↗

Private schools have separate Day and Boarding alternatives under the same school ID when an offering and price are verified. A missing boarding option means that alternative is unverified, not that boarding is impossible. Boarding keeps the family at its current home: annual cost = current-home spending + boarding package and fees + known travel. The editable current-home default is the $74,326 BLS three-person benchmark before school. It remains unchanged when a child boards; no food or utility savings are assumed, so reduce it for your own circumstances. Boarding is plotted by annual cost, with lower cost to the right. Campus livability and campus rent do not establish the family’s home value. Unknown travel and required charges retain a model cost floor (≥), not a guaranteed minimum bill. Five-day boarding and holiday arrangements require separate planning.

Published tuition years vary and appear in profiles. Unpriced required charges create value ceilings (≤) for day comparisons; modeled ranges retain their endpoints. Undated rates are explicitly conditional assumptions. Aid, moving expenses, optional charges, first-time admission fees and sibling discounts are not assumed. The AARP community score already contains affordability measures; dividing by cost emphasizes affordability again. Review school attendance, cost sources and limits ↗

Comparison limits, boundaries, and source dates

A complete ranked prefix. Qualifying ranks #1–#100 are positions in this admission-filtered list. National ranks retain their original numbers. This category is defined by School Atlas; U.S. News ranks all included public-school types together. Each school tab or private attendance view uses linear axes fitted to its full group, with padding and rounded tick bounds. Value or annual-cost ranges include finite uncertainty interval endpoints. The axes cross at the midpoint of each displayed range; these are visual references, not cohort medians. Scales change between school tabs, so equal distances across tabs do not represent equal numerical differences. City and state filters keep the active tab’s scale, including when no schools match. Rank, value and dollar amounts retain their original meanings; public and private ranks remain separate publisher rankings. Unavailable schools remain searchable in the list and are excluded from the graph.

Housing and safety. The AARP inputs combine different years and geographies. The retrieved crime input uses a 2016 county measure, and major housing-cost inputs use 2019–2023 ACS data. Scores should not be interpreted as current neighborhood crime or the price of a new rental. Climate preferences, specific restaurants, and an individual commute are not modeled.

Geography and enrollment. Cities, townships, and Census-designated places are community proxies, not attendance boundaries. Lambert uses the larger Forsyth County geography. Exact address, entry year, capacity, proof of residence, and specialized-program rules can affect enrollment; profiles retain school-specific notes.

Ranking provenance. Official district sources support the admission screening. Exact national ranks use HighSchoolTrends’ republication because the complete primary list was inaccessible. We checked the complete ordered sequence against its state tables and API; these are two presentations of the same secondary source, not independent publisher verification. School-specific primary confirmations are labeled separately. New York also has a missing-AP-data limitation in this edition. Read the reporting ↗
