# Where a Family’s Dollar Buys More

*A school search must include the cost of the life around it.*

Editorial draft for Substack. The publication and final article URL have not been selected.

School rankings answer one question: where does a school stand in a publisher’s list?

A family has a larger question. What will life near that school cost, and what will that spending buy?

The school is one part of that choice. Rent, daily travel, the surrounding community, and tuition can change the comparison.

An expensive place can have excellent schools. That alone does not show that it offers good value for a family.

I built School Atlas to put these questions on the same screen. It compares school rank with community quality and estimated family costs.

The graph keeps school rank separate from value. Moving up means a better position in the selected publisher’s ranking. Moving right means a higher Value index for public and private day schools.

For boarding schools, moving right means a lower estimated annual family cost. Boarding uses a separate cost comparison because the family can remain at home.

## Three different ways into a school

The first distinction is admission.

School Atlas has three groups, each with 100 schools:

- **Local public:** schools with documented ordinary residential entry at high-school level, without a lottery or selective admission.
- **Lottery/selection:** public schools with a lottery or selective entry route.
- **Private:** campus-based private schools with application requirements and published cost evidence.

The groups are separate. A school appears in one group only. Online schools are excluded.

These are the first 100 qualifying schools in rank order within each group. Their original publisher ranks remain visible.

A local public school can have a national rank above 100. Higher-ranked schools can belong to the lottery or selective group.

The public-school ranks come from the U.S. News 2026–2027 edition. Exact positions mainly use HighSchoolTrends’ republication, with source links and admission evidence retained.

That distinction matters. Checking two presentations of a secondary source is not independent confirmation from the ranking publisher.

Private schools use Niche’s separate 2027 ranking. A private rank of 20 and a public rank of 20 describe different ranking populations.

## What the Value index means

The Value index estimates community quality relative to annual family spending.

It uses an equal-weight average of six AARP categories: housing, neighborhood, transportation, environment, health, and engagement. This is a community proxy, not a school quality score.

The formula is:

**Value index = community score × 64,877 ÷ estimated annual family cost**

The fixed factor makes the result easier to read. It changes the scale, not the order of places.

The result has no dollar unit. It is not a percentile or a score out of 100.

Consider a community score of 60 and an annual family cost of $100,000. The resulting index is 38.9.

At $80,000, the same community score produces an index of 48.7. The community quality is unchanged, while the estimated spending is lower.

School rank does not enter this formula. A reader can assess academic position and community value separately on the graph.

## Start with the home a family needs

A metropolitan price index can hide large differences in local housing costs. A family also needs a concrete housing assumption.

School Atlas starts with a three-bedroom rental. It uses the school campus ZIP and FY2027 HUD gross-rent benchmarks, which include covered utilities.

The ZIP is a nearby housing proxy. It is not an attendance boundary or evidence that a suitable rental is available today.

For public and private day schools, annual family cost includes:

- Twelve months of the local three-bedroom rent benchmark.
- Other household spending.
- Tuition and known school fees for the selected number of children.
- Known household school fees, added once.

The default for other household spending is $52,956. It comes from BLS 2024 spending for three-person households, after specified exclusions.

Shelter and covered utilities are removed before local rent is added. Education, cash contributions, and personal insurance and pensions are also excluded.

Food, transportation, health and vehicle insurance, phone and internet, and household goods remain in that benchmark. These costs stay at national 2024 prices.

The default is a common comparison basis. It is not a researched bill for a particular family. Readers can change the assumptions and number of children.

## Tuition belongs in the total

For a private day school, tuition must be part of the comparison. A low rent cannot erase a large school bill.

The model includes sourced tuition and known required fees before financial aid. Published years and unresolved charges remain visible in the school card.

Day and boarding are separate alternatives. The private catalog has 100 unique schools, with 96 priced day alternatives and 32 priced boarding alternatives.

Some schools offer both. Those alternatives do not turn one school into two schools.

For boarding, the default family assumption is to remain at the current home. The model adds the boarding package and known travel to home spending.

The default home benchmark is $74,326 per year. It does not automatically assume lower food or utility costs while a child boards.

Campus rent is excluded from that total. Campus community scores do not represent the family’s home, so boarding uses annual dollars rather than a family Value index.

## Uncertainty should stay visible

A precise-looking number can still rest on incomplete prices.

School Atlas marks estimates with **≈**. A hollow point marks unpriced costs.

For day schools, missing required charges create a Value index ceiling, marked **≤**. Additional spending can reduce the final value.

For boarding, the same issue creates a known cost floor, marked **≥**. The final bill can be higher.

Unknown travel is not treated as a verified zero. Published cost ranges and five-day boarding arrangements also remain visible.

Financial aid, moving costs, and optional charges can change a family’s actual result. The community inputs also combine different years and geographic areas.

The AARP community score includes affordability measures. Dividing it by family cost puts additional weight on affordability.

Those limits are part of the model, not footnotes to remove for a cleaner graph.

## A better starting point for a family decision

School Atlas helps identify places worth examining more closely. It does not replace an address check, an admission inquiry, or a current school quote.

The next useful step is specific: select a school, inspect its costs and sources, and check the assumptions against the family’s plans.

The goal is to find places where the family’s dollar buys more of the life it wants.

## Sources and method

This draft describes the School Atlas snapshot assembled on October 20, 2026. Ranking editions, rent years, tuition years, and community input years differ.

- [U.S. News public-school ranking publisher](https://www.usnews.com/education/best-high-schools/national-rankings).
- [HighSchoolTrends public rank tables](https://highschooltrends.org/atlas), the principal source for transcribed national positions.
- [Niche 2027 private-school ranking](https://www.niche.com/k12/search/best-private-high-schools/?page=1).
- [AARP Livability Index](https://livabilityindex.aarp.org/), the source of the six community categories.
- [HUD Small Area Fair Market Rents](https://www.huduser.gov/portal/datasets/fmr/smallarea/index.html), the source of local three-bedroom rent benchmarks.
- [BLS 2024 household spending table](https://www.bls.gov/cex/tables/calendar-year/mean-item-share-average-standard-error/cu-size-2024.xlsx), the source of the household spending benchmarks.

The application contains the full formula, source links, admission audit, cost evidence, and comparison limits.
